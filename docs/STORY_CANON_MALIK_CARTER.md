# OVERCHARGE — CANON STORY: MALIK CARTER
**Status:** CANON CORE approved by Chief, 2026-10-14. Expanded narrative below is a development direction, not locked canon until Chief approves.
**Authority:** This document supersedes conflicting hero-origin proposals in `docs/STORY_BIBLE_DRAFT.md`. Do not treat old draft premise options as chosen.

## 1. LOCKED CANON — Chief's premise
- The protagonist is **Malik Carter**, **17 years old**.
- Malik is a regular kid growing up in an **inner-city neighborhood**.
- One day, during a **strange electrical storm**, Malik is **struck by lightning**.
- The storm is **artificial**, caused by **XXX conducting weather-manipulation testing**. **XXX is a placeholder**, not an approved organization name.
- This incident is Malik's superhero origin and the foundation of OVERCHARGE's story.
- Tone target: original, grounded, street-level coming-of-age superhero adventure with neighborhood identity, humor, and serious consequences. Do not copy Static Shock characters or plots.

## 2. EXPANDED STORY DIRECTION — proposed, not yet locked

### Before the storm
Malik Carter is seventeen, clever with electronics but more interested in getting through school, helping at home, and hanging with his friends than saving the world. He knows the shortcuts, corner stores, apartment roofs, and people on his block. He has no idea that the skyline above his neighborhood has become a test range.

The city has suffered increasingly unnatural weather: clear skies that suddenly crackle with thunder, rain falling in perfectly straight corridors, and power outages confined to just a few streets. Official reports call them rare atmospheric anomalies. Residents know something isn't right.

### The night everything changed
During a summer evening, clouds assemble too quickly over Malik's district. Streetlights flash blue. Phone screens glitch. Thunder hits before the rain starts. Malik is outside when an impossible bolt splits into several branches, strikes a utility structure, and arcs directly into him.

For a moment, the entire block goes dark. Malik should be dead. Instead, he wakes on the pavement to the sound of every nearby transformer humming in rhythm with his heartbeat. His hands spark. A broken streetlamp turns on when he touches it; another goes dark as its charge flows into him.

### What really happened
XXX has been testing technology intended to **steer and intensify storms** by manipulating atmospheric charge. Publicly, the program promises drought relief, disaster prevention, and predictable rainfall. Secretly, its operators have been experimenting with concentrating electrical discharges over precise geographic targets.

The test was not supposed to strike a person. Malik's survival is unprecedented. The lightning carried an unusual, engineered charge profile, and his nervous system somehow adapted instead of shutting down. He can now **absorb, store, move, and discharge electricity**, but the amount he can safely hold is limited.

### The first conflict
Malik doesn't immediately put on a costume. He tries to hide it. His first experiments are clumsy: a vending machine shorts out, a streetlight flickers, a locked electrical gate responds to his touch. As he learns to carry charge from one source to another, the player learns the same rule: **power is finite; using it here means not having it somewhere else.**

Then he notices unmarked maintenance crews replacing equipment after each storm. Cameras follow him. Someone has access to footage of the lightning strike, and the footage proves the storm was no accident.

### A larger threat
The city has been divided into weather-test zones without residents' knowledge or consent. Malik's neighborhood was selected because the people running the program believed the consequences would be easy to dismiss as routine infrastructure failure. The experiment is about to be repeated on a much larger scale.

Malik isn't fighting to save a faceless world. He's fighting to stop his own streets from becoming the next test site.

### Proposed central mystery and reveal
Why did Malik survive? Was it chance, something in the engineered lightning, or a hidden variable in the experiment? Keep the answer unresolved until later in the story. Avoid declaring Malik a chosen one or introducing a secret bloodline without Chief's approval.

## 3. Gameplay and world connections — proposed
- **Absorb:** Malik pulls electrical charge from street infrastructure and machines.
- **Carry:** Charge is limited and must be protected during traversal.
- **Spend:** He powers gates, switches, equipment, and neighborhood systems.
- **Drones/cameras:** Can be explained as test-site monitoring hardware once approved.
- **Level 1 / Neon Rise:** Can introduce Malik's first attempts to control his power, but do not rewrite the existing tutorial, source count, or 8-charge exit requirement without approval.
- **NPCs:** Mechanics, neighbors, and street characters provide clues through short dialogue, keeping the setting alive without requiring a cutscene system.
