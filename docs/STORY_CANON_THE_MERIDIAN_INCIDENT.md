# OVERCHARGE — THE MERIDIAN INCIDENT
**Status: OFFICIAL STORY CANON — Chief approved full six-chapter narrative, 2026-10-14.**
**Canonical names:** **Meridian Industries** (parent corporation) → **MIOR: Meridian Institute of Research** (research division) → **PROJECT TEMPEST** (classified weather-manipulation experiment).
**Continuity:** This document supersedes earlier story brainstorms and abbreviated origin pitches. The six chapters and “Where the story goes next” are approved story direction. No game-code, level, NPC-dialogue or asset changes are authorized by this story-only commit.

## Premise

Meridian Industries is a private corporation with government contracts, multiple divisions, and experimental facilities across the country. Its research arm, the **Meridian Institute of Research (MIOR)**, develops technologies meant to improve everyday life. Behind closed doors, its researchers push the boundaries of what science can control.

MIOR isn't exclusively an energy research institute. Weather manipulation is simply one experiment among hundreds.

## Chapter 1 — The Storm

Seventeen-year-old Malik Carter grew up in the inner city, surrounded by people who knew his family, neighborhood basketball courts, corner stores, and apartment buildings that had seen better days.

Malik was an ordinary teenager. He went to school, hung out with his friends, got into occasional trouble, and spent his free time messing with electronics.

What Malik didn't know was that his neighborhood had been selected for a classified atmospheric experiment conducted by the Meridian Institute of Research (MIOR).

MIOR had been developing a technology capable of manipulating atmospheric conditions. Their goal was to control the formation, movement, and intensity of weather systems.

The experiment required a populated environment to study how the technology performed around buildings, electrical infrastructure, and everyday human activity.

Malik's neighborhood was chosen because the people running the program believed that, if anything went wrong, the consequences could be dismissed as routine infrastructure failures.

One evening, an unnatural storm rolled across the neighborhood.

The sky turned an unusual shade of violet. Lightning flashed between clouds without thunder. Streetlights flickered, radios crackled, and the air seemed to vibrate.

Then came a lightning strike unlike anything the city had ever seen.

Malik was caught directly in its path.

For several seconds, every light on the block went out.

When the electricity returned, Malik was lying unconscious in the street.

He survived.

But something inside him had changed.

## Chapter 2 — Something Different

Over the following weeks, Malik begins experiencing things he can't explain.

His phone charges while sitting in his hand. Streetlights brighten when he walks beneath them. When he gets angry, nearby electronics malfunction.

Eventually, he discovers that he can absorb electricity from powered objects, store it inside his body, and release it.

He doesn't understand why. And he's terrified of telling anybody.

Meanwhile, MIOR has already completed its atmospheric testing and moved on.

The storm was one entry in a much larger catalog of research. The scientists recorded an unexpected electrical discharge, but they had no reason to believe a teenager had developed superhuman abilities.

**As far as MIOR is concerned, Malik Carter doesn't exist.**

## Chapter 3 — The Logo

Months later, Malik is walking through his neighborhood when he spots an unfamiliar vehicle parked outside a building undergoing renovations.

The vehicle bears a distinctive symbol: three narrow silver lines converging toward a single point.

Beneath it are the words:

**MERIDIAN INSTITUTE OF RESEARCH**

Malik recognizes the symbol.

He remembers seeing it on equipment trucks in his neighborhood the morning after the storm.

At the time, he assumed they were utility workers repairing damage.

Now he's curious.

He takes a picture and starts looking into the organization.

Its website is filled with polished videos about medical research, robotics, advanced materials, atmospheric science, and next-generation technology.

Nothing particularly suspicious.

Until Malik discovers that the building being renovated isn't listed among Meridian's public facilities.

And the work is being done at night.

## Chapter 4 — The Drones

A few weeks later, the city announces a partnership with MIOR to introduce autonomous public-safety drones.

The initiative is marketed as a breakthrough in emergency response.

The drones can identify accidents, detect fires, locate missing persons, and assist police with surveillance.

At first, people love them.

Videos of drones helping firefighters and locating lost children spread across social media.

But Malik notices something strange.

One evening, while practicing his abilities on an abandoned rooftop, a drone stops in the middle of its patrol.

Its camera rotates toward him.

Malik ducks behind a wall.

The drone remains motionless for several seconds before continuing along its route.

He assumes it was a coincidence.

Until it happens again.

And again.

The drones are designed to recognize unusual electromagnetic activity as part of their hazard-detection systems. Malik's powers produce an electrical signature they have never encountered.

But the drones aren't specifically hunting Malik, and their operators don't yet know what is triggering the alerts.

To MIOR, it's just another unexplained sensor reading.

To Malik, it's the first indication that using his powers might expose him.

## Chapter 5 — The First Clue

Malik's curiosity about MIOR becomes an obsession.

He begins noticing its name in places he never paid attention to before.

A robotics demonstration at a nearby university. A new medical research wing. Experimental traffic-control equipment. A technology grant awarded to his school.

Meridian seems to have its hands in everything.

Then a local news story catches his attention.

A former MIOR researcher has disappeared after claiming that the institute conducted unauthorized human-exposure experiments.

The researcher had been trying to release evidence of a classified project before vanishing.

The project name?

**PROJECT TEMPEST.**

Malik searches for it.

Nothing.

No research papers. No government filings. No public records.

But buried in an old photograph from a neighborhood community meeting, Malik spots a document on a table.

In the corner of the document is a small printed label:

**MIOR / PROJECT TEMPEST / FIELD OPERATIONS**

The photograph was taken three days before the storm.

For the first time, Malik has evidence connecting Meridian to the night he was struck.

And now he wants answers.

## Chapter 6 — The Wrong Place, the Wrong Time

Malik tracks down the last known workplace of the missing researcher: a Meridian storage and maintenance facility on the outskirts of the city.

He isn't planning to fight anybody.

He wants to find out what Project Tempest was and why the researcher disappeared.

Using his electrical abilities, Malik slips past an electronically locked service entrance.

Inside, he finds crates of experimental equipment, dismantled drone components, and archived research materials.

He eventually discovers a terminal containing a partial record of Project Tempest.

The experiment was conducted in his neighborhood.

The weather event was artificially generated.

And the lightning strike was recorded as an **unanticipated discharge event**.

Malik finally understands what happened to him.

Before he can learn anything else, the building's security system detects an unauthorized entry.

A drone activates.

Then another.

Malik tries to escape, using his powers to disable the machines.

But the confrontation triggers an automated security report.

The report includes footage of a teenager manipulating electricity with his bare hands.

For the first time, someone at Meridian sees something they cannot explain.

**The storm didn't just produce an electrical anomaly. It created Malik.**

And the institute has just discovered that its experiment did something nobody thought possible.

## Where the story goes next

Meridian's ultimate objective remains a mystery for now rather than immediately revealing a plan to control the city.

The stronger long-term conflict is that Meridian operates dozens of research programs. Some are genuinely beneficial. Others are ethically questionable. A few are extremely dangerous.

Malik initially wants answers about his own powers. But the deeper he investigates, the more he uncovers about the organization's experiments—and the people willing to protect them.

That allows us to introduce different villains, technology, locations, and abilities without making every storyline about electricity.

**Antagonist direction:** The first major antagonist is Meridian's head of security, not its CEO. That gives Malik a dangerous opponent while the people truly responsible remain out of reach.

## Continuity / implementation notes

- The company does **not** know Malik survived with powers until the Chapter 6 security footage. The earlier drone alerts are anomalous readings, **not** a targeted hunt.
- MIOR has already **moved on** from the neighborhood after the weather trial; it is not running a citywide blackout plot or electrical-grid mapping operation.
- Preserve the established game mechanic: Malik absorbs, stores, and spends limited electricity.
- Story canon does not automatically authorize rewriting Level 1 gameplay, its three four-charge sources, eight-charge exit cost, or existing NPCs.
- Open for later naming: city, neighborhood, missing researcher, security chief, and individual MIOR facilities.
